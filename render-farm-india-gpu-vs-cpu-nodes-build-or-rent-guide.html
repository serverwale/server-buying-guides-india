Building a Render Farm in India: GPU vs CPU Nodes, Sizing and Buy vs Rent

A render farm is a group of computers that render frames while your artists keep working. For a studio in India the real choice is between building your own nodes, renting nodes by the month, or paying a cloud service per frame. This guide helps you size the farm, pick GPU or CPU nodes and decide whether to own one. Serverwale's render farm page shows reference builds, and its cloud workstation page covers remote GPU desktops for artists.

Step 2: size the farm from your deadline

  1. Time one representative frame on one node. Use your hardest scene, not an average one.
  2. Multiply by the number of frames in your longest job.
  3. Divide by the hours you can allow. The result is how many nodes you need, before a safety margin for failures and re-renders.
  4. Add 20 to 30 percent headroom as a planning assumption of ours, not a Serverwale figure.

Example, purely illustrative: a frame takes 12 minutes on one node, the job has 3,000 frames and you can allow 48 hours. Total work is 36,000 minutes or 600 node-hours, so about 13 nodes at full use, before headroom. Always test your own scenes first.

Step 3: do not forget storage and network

Nodes sit idle if they wait for textures and caches. Serverwale's page says its builds integrate NAS storage with 10GbE or 25GbE networking. Plan shared storage with our NAS buying guide, SAN vs NAS and network card speed guide. Serverwale's NAS rental page lists storage options. Keep a real backup, because RAID is not a backup.

Step 4: power, cooling and rack space

A GPU node can draw several hundred watts under load. Check circuits and UPS capacity with the UPS sizing guide and PDU guide, and plan airflow using the cooling guide. If nodes will sit in a studio room rather than a data room, noise and heat matter as much as price.

Checklist before you buy render nodes

  • Render engine and version supported, and a test of your own scene on the exact GPU or CPU.
  • VRAM or RAM per node versus your largest scene.
  • Render manager included (Serverwale names Deadline, RenderPal and OpenCue) and who installs it.
  • Shared storage capacity and network speed written into the quote.
  • Warranty length, who replaces a failed GPU and how fast. Read warranty and testing.
  • Power and cooling check done before delivery day.

Frequently asked questions

How many render nodes does a small studio need? Time one hard frame, multiply by your frame count and divide by the hours you have. Serverwale lists a 4-node GPU starter for small studios, but your own timing decides the number.

Is a GPU or CPU render farm better? GPU nodes suit GPU engines such as Redshift, Octane or Cycles if the scene fits in VRAM. CPU nodes suit CPU engines and very large scenes that need lots of system RAM.

Is an on-premise render farm cheaper than cloud rendering? It can be when you render steadily every month, because you stop paying per frame. Serverwale claims 70 to 90 percent lower per-frame cost; for a rare job, cloud or monthly rental may still cost less.

Can I rent render nodes instead of buying? Serverwale says nodes can be bought or rented monthly. Ask about minimum term, deposit and whether rental can convert to purchase.

What storage does a render farm need? Fast shared storage so nodes do not wait for assets, typically a NAS on 10GbE or faster, plus a separate backup copy.

Do artists need local workstations if I have a farm? Usually yes for interactive work, or they can use remote GPU workstations. The farm handles final frames, the workstation handles scene setup.
