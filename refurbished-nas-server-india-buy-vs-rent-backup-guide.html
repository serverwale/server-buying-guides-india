Refurbished NAS Server in India: Size It, Buy or Rent It, Back It Up

For a small office that needs shared files and a place to keep backups, a refurbished NAS is usually the cheapest sensible answer: a 2 to 4 bay unit for a team of a few people, an enterprise rack NAS such as a Dell PowerVault NX for a department, and a high-bay storage server for archives and video. Rent instead of buying when the need is temporary: a project, a migration or a short-term backup copy. Whatever you choose, a NAS is only part of a backup plan, not all of it. This guide helps you size it, decide between renting and buying a NAS server, and avoid the usual mistakes.

Step 1: size the capacity honestly

  1. Add up the data you have now.
  2. Add the growth you expect over three years. Many offices see yearly growth, so do not size for today only.
  3. Subtract what RAID uses. RAID 1 mirrors, so you keep about half of raw space. RAID 5 gives up one drive, RAID 6 gives up two. The RAID levels guide explains the trade-offs.
  4. If the NAS will also hold backups, add the backup copies on top. A backup of 4 TB of data kept for several versions needs more than 4 TB.

Example, purely illustrative: 6 TB of files, expected to double in three years, needs about 12 TB usable. In RAID 6 on 6 drives of 4 TB, you keep 4 x 4 = 16 TB usable before any backup copies. That is simple arithmetic, not a quote.

Step 2: RAID is not backup

RAID keeps the NAS running when a drive fails. It does not help if someone deletes a folder, ransomware encrypts the shares, a controller fails or the office floods. Plan for the common 3-2-1 approach: three copies of your data, on two different kinds of storage, with one copy off-site. A NAS can be one of those copies, or the primary storage that gets backed up. Read RAID is not a backup and, for a bigger picture of how the pieces fit, the SAN vs NAS guide. If the NAS is also where servers keep data, see NAS vs SAN vs DAS.

Step 4: what to check before paying

  • Drives: are they included, what type and capacity, and what is their health? For SSDs use the remaining-life guide; for pricing see the drive price guide.
  • RAID controller or software RAID: know which one it uses; see RAID controller prices.
  • Network ports: 1GbE is fine for a few users; a busy NAS or backup target benefits from 10GbE. See the NIC speed guide and NIC teaming.
  • Power: dual power supplies on rack models, plus a UPS (UPS sizing).
  • Operating system and licence: Windows Storage Server units need a valid licence; TrueNAS and Linux NAS do not. See server licensing.
  • Support: warranty, replacement process and AMC. See server AMC cost, warranty and testing and what refurbished means.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a refurbished NAS cost in India? Serverwale's NAS page shows indicative starting prices of about ₹8,790 for a 2 to 4 bay Buffalo or QNAP unit and about ₹20,694 for a Dell PowerVault NX rack NAS, excluding GST. Final price depends on bays, capacity and drives.

Can I rent a NAS instead of buying? Yes. Serverwale states that NAS rental is available with a one-month minimum and includes delivery and configuration. Renting suits projects, migrations and temporary backup copies.

Is a NAS a backup? Not by itself. RAID protects against a failed drive, not deletion, ransomware or a site disaster. Keep at least one extra copy on different storage and one off-site.

Which RAID level should I use on a NAS? RAID 5 or RAID 6 give more usable space for file storage; RAID 6 survives two drive failures; RAID 1 or 10 suit smaller or write-heavy setups. Pick by how much capacity and protection you need.

NAS or SAN: which do I need? A NAS shares files over the network and suits file shares and backups. A SAN gives block storage to servers and suits virtual machine datastores.
