Server SSD vs SAS HDD in India: Which to Buy for Each Workload

For a server, use enterprise SSDs for data that is read and written constantly (operating system, databases, virtual machines, cache) and SAS or nearline HDDs for data that is large and mostly sequential (backups, archives, video and CCTV retention). Most servers end up with both, tiered by how hot the data is. This guide compares the two with published datasheet figures, explains endurance ratings, and shows how to choose drives for each workload. It supports the server SSD vs HDD comparison on Serverwale and is written for IT buyers in India sizing storage for new or refurbished servers.

What DWPD and TBW mean, and why they matter for SSDs

SSD flash wears with writes, so enterprise SSDs state an endurance rating. DWPD (drive writes per day) is how much of the drive’s capacity can be written each day over the warranty period; TBW (terabytes written) is the lifetime total. They convert: TBW = DWPD x capacity in TB x warranty years x 365. Endurance is a prediction, not a guarantee, and depends on workload and write amplification (see the SNIA and Micron endurance papers). A mostly-read workload such as a file server or web content suits a read-intensive SSD; a write-heavy database needs a higher-endurance drive.

For refurbished drives, the rating is only half the story: you also need to know how much life is already used. NVMe drives report a “percentage used” value in their SMART log. Compatibility checks before you buy drives

  • Controller and backplane: confirm the server’s RAID or HBA controller and backplane support SAS, SATA or NVMe drives of the type you plan to use.
  • Caddies: branded servers use drive caddies. Serverwale states that it supplies drives with genuine OEM caddies for Dell, HP, IBM/Lenovo and Supermicro systems.
  • Form factor: 2.5-inch and 3.5-inch bays differ, so match capacity needs to the bay type.
  • Drive health: ask for SMART data on any used drive, and a written warranty.

Frequently asked questions

Is an SSD always better than an HDD in a server?

No. SSDs are far faster for random reads and writes and have lower latency, which suits databases, virtual machines and boot volumes. HDDs cost less per terabyte and give more capacity per drive, which suits backups, archives and video retention. Most servers use both.

What does DWPD mean on an enterprise SSD?

DWPD means drive writes per day: how many times the full capacity of the drive can be written each day over the warranty period. TBW is the lifetime total, and TBW equals DWPD times capacity in terabytes times warranty years times 365. Both are predictions that depend on workload.

How do I check how much life a refurbished SSD has left?

Read the drive's SMART data. NVMe drives report a percentage-used value in the SMART log, and other drives expose wear indicators that vary by vendor. Ask the seller for the SMART report and a written warranty before buying a used SSD.

Which is better for a database server, SAS HDD or SSD?

An enterprise SSD. Databases depend on random IOPS and low latency, where flash is far ahead of a 10K SAS disk. Keep backups on lower-cost HDDs.

Can I mix SSDs and HDDs in one server?

Often yes, if the controller and backplane support both. A common design puts the OS and hot data on SSDs and bulk data on HDDs, or uses SSDs as a cache or tier in front of HDDs. Confirm support for your exact server and controller.
