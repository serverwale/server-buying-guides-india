Refurbished HP Z Workstations in India: Which Z240, Z440, Z640, Z840 or G4 Model to Buy

HP's Z workstations come in two families that are easy to confuse. The older ones, the Z240, Z440, Z640 and Z840, use Intel Xeon E3 or E5 processors. The newer Z4, Z6 and Z8 G4 use Intel Xeon W or Xeon Scalable processors, and the Z2 Mini is a small desktop. The right pick depends on what you run: single-thread speed for modelling, many cores for rendering, memory for simulation, or small size for a desk. This guide compares them using the specifications on Serverwale's product pages and lists what to check before you pay for a refurbished workstation.

Older Z family or newer G4 family?

The Z240 to Z840 are the budget route and are still used for CAD, editing and rendering. The G4 models (Z4, Z6, Z8) are newer, use Xeon W or Xeon Scalable processors and support more memory. A Z840 gives you many cores on the older platform, while a Z4 G4 gives a newer single-socket platform and a Z8 G4 newer dual-socket processors. If you are comparing a workstation with a server, our workstations for rendering guide and the Xeon vs EPYC note explain where each fits, and Intel Xeon processor prices help you judge upgrades.

ECC memory or not

Serverwale's Z240 page lists DDR4 ECC or non-ECC memory depending on the processor, while the Z440, Z640, Z840 and the G4 models take ECC registered memory. ECC corrects single-bit errors and suits long renders and simulations where one bad bit could ruin hours of work. Read the RAM buying guide and the DDR4 price guide before you upgrade.

Graphics, power and storage: what to check

  • Graphics card: ask which card is fitted, how much video memory it has and whether the power supply has the connectors for it. A card that does not fit the power supply is a common surprise. See the GPU workstation guide for AI and rendering use.
  • Power supply: a workstation with two processors and a large GPU needs a bigger supply than a single-socket model. Match the supply to the parts, and add a UPS if power is unstable (UPS sizing).
  • Storage: SSD or NVMe for the system and active projects, a hard drive for archives; the SSD remaining-life guide shows how to check used drives.
  • Software certification: HP lists software certifications per model and configuration. If you run CAD or media software that needs certified hardware, check the certification for the exact setup.
  • Warranty and testing: Serverwale states a 1-year hardware warranty on its hardware; read the terms and see warranty and testing.

Frequently asked questions

Which HP Z workstation is best for CAD? For CAD and moderate 3D, a single-socket Z440 or Z4 G4 with a professional graphics card is usually enough because modelling relies on single-thread speed. Choose a dual-socket model only if you also render or simulate.

What is the difference between the HP Z440, Z640 and Z840? The Z440 is single-socket. The Z640 takes one or two Xeon E5 processors (up to 44 cores in total according to Serverwale's listing), and the Z840 is a dual-socket model with 16 memory slots. All use DDR4 ECC memory.

Are the HP Z4, Z6 and Z8 G4 newer than the Z840? Yes. The G4 models use Intel Xeon W (Z4) or Xeon Scalable (Z6, Z8) processors and support more memory than the older Z240 to Z840 range.

Does the HP Z240 support ECC memory? Serverwale's Z240 page lists DDR4 ECC or non-ECC memory, depending on the processor fitted. Check the processor in the exact unit.

Can I rent an HP Z workstation instead of buying? Yes. Serverwale lists rental options such as the HP Z440 and Z640. Renting suits render deadlines, short AI projects and temporary teams.

What warranty comes with a refurbished workstation from Serverwale? Serverwale states a 1-year hardware warranty on its hardware. Confirm the exact coverage and terms in writing before you order.
